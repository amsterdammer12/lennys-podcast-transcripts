# Fireside Chat — Language Toolkit for Non-Native Speakers
## Simple phrases that sound professional and create depth

---

## Golden Rule

**Short sentences are powerful sentences.** Native speakers often use too many words. As a non-native speaker, your directness is a *strength*, not a weakness. The best interviewers (including Lenny) use simple language.

---

## 1. Opening the Conversation

Don't try to be fancy. Be warm and direct.

| Instead of... | Say this |
|---|---|
| "It's an honor and a privilege to have both of you here with us today for this incredibly exciting conversation" | **"I'm really excited about this conversation. Let's jump in."** |
| "I'd like to begin by asking you to introduce yourselves" | **"Tanya, Ash — tell us: what do you actually do every day?"** |

### Your Opening (memorize one)

> "So — AI is changing how we build. Data is changing how we grow. And the line between product and marketing? It's disappearing. Tanya and Ash are right in the middle of this. Let's talk about it."

Short. Clear. Sets the stage. Done.

---

## 2. Asking Questions — Keep Them Short

The best questions are **one sentence**. Two at most.

### Pattern: "What + specific thing"

- "What does that actually look like in practice?"
- "What surprised you most about that?"
- "What would you do differently now?"
- "What's the biggest mistake you see people make here?"

### Pattern: "How + you/your team"

- "How did you figure that out?"
- "How does your team decide what to build next?"
- "How do you measure if it's working?"

### Pattern: "Why" (this creates gentle pressure)

- "Why do you think that is?"
- "Why does that matter?"
- "Why don't more companies do this?"

---

## 3. Going Deeper — Gentle Pressure Phrases

This is what you really need. These phrases push the speaker to go beyond their rehearsed answer — politely but firmly.

### Level 1: Friendly push

| Phrase | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **"Can you be more specific?"** | When the answer is too abstract |
| **"What do you mean by that?"** | When they use jargon or buzzwords |
| **"Give me an example."** | When you need something concrete |
| **"Say more about that."** | When they touched on something interesting but moved on |

### Level 2: Constructive challenge

| Phrase | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **"I hear that a lot. But does it actually work?"** | When the answer sounds like a conference talk |
| **"That sounds great in theory. What goes wrong in practice?"** | When the answer is too optimistic |
| **"Some people would disagree with that. What would you say to them?"** | When you want to test their conviction |
| **"Is that really true? Or is that what we like to believe?"** | When you want to push harder (use sparingly) |

### Level 3: The polite redirect

| Phrase | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **"That's interesting — but I want to go back to something you said earlier..."** | When they're going off-topic |
| **"I want to push you on this a bit."** | Announces pressure, so it doesn't feel aggressive |
| **"I don't fully buy that yet. Convince me."** | Strong but playful — smile when you say it |
| **"OK, but what about [the hard part]?"** | Redirects from the easy answer to the real answer |

### The Magic Phrase: "I want to push you on this a bit"

This is your best friend. It:
- Warns the speaker that a harder question is coming (so it feels fair)
- Shows the audience you're in control
- Sounds confident and professional
- Is only 9 words

> "That's a great point, Ash. But I want to push you on this a bit — if anyone can build an app now, does quality even matter anymore?"

---

## 4. Active Listening — Phrases to Connect Answers

These short phrases show you're listening AND move the conversation forward.

### Confirming you understood

- **"So basically..."** + your one-sentence summary
- **"In other words..."** + your restatement
- **"Let me make sure I got that..."** + short version

### Bridging between speakers

- **"Tanya, same question to you."**
- **"Ash, what's your take on that?"**
- **"Interesting — because Tanya just said [X]. How does that fit with your experience, Ash?"**
- **"You're both saying [X], but from completely different angles."**

### Reacting naturally (keep it short)

- **"That's a really good point."**
- **"I didn't expect that answer."**
- **"OK, that's interesting."**
- **"Wait — really?"** (shows genuine surprise)

---

## 5. Managing Time and Transitions

### Changing topics

- **"Let's switch gears."**
- **"I want to talk about something different."**
- **"OK — new topic."**

### Cutting someone off (politely)

- **"I'm going to stop you there — because that's exactly what I wanted to ask about next."**
- **"I love this topic, but I want to make sure we get to [X]."**
- **"Hold that thought — we'll come back to it."**

### Keeping answers short

- **"Give me the short version."**
- **"In one sentence?"**
- **"If you had to pick just one thing?"**

---

## 6. Handling Moments You Don't Understand

This happens to everyone — native speakers too. The key: **don't pretend you understood**.

| Situation | What to say |
|---|---|
| You didn't catch a word | **"Can you explain what you mean by [word]?"** |
| The answer was too complex | **"Can you say that in simpler terms? I want to make sure the audience follows."** |
| You're lost | **"OK, I want to pause here. Help me understand..."** |
| A technical term came up | **"For people who don't know — what is [term]?"** |

**Pro tip**: Blame the audience, not yourself. "I want to make sure *everyone* follows" sounds better than "I don't understand." But honestly? Saying "I don't fully get that — can you break it down?" makes you look confident, not weak.

---

## 7. The Lightning Round — Exact Script

This is the easiest part. Memorize these and fire them off quickly.

> "OK — lightning round. Short answers only."
>
> "Favorite product you've discovered recently?"
>
> "Most overrated buzzword in tech right now?"
>
> "In two years, what will be completely different about how we build products?"
>
> "Best advice you ever got?"

---

## 8. Closing — Keep It Simple

> "Tanya, Ash — thank you. This was a great conversation."
>
> "Where can people find you online?"
>
> *(After they answer)*
>
> "Let's give them a hand."

That's it. Don't over-complicate the close.

---

## 9. Emergency Phrases — When Things Go Wrong

| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Awkward silence | **"Take your time."** (Then wait. Silence is OK.) |
| Both talk at once | **"Ash, go ahead — Tanya, I'll come to you next."** |
| Answer goes on too long | **"I'm going to jump in here..."** |
| You forget your next question | **"Actually, something you just said made me curious..."** (buys you time to think) |
| Technical problem | **"Let's give it a second."** (Stay calm, smile) |
| You lose your train of thought | **"Where were we? Right —"** (natural, everyone does this) |

---

## 10. Practice Routine (Before the Event)

1. **Read this document twice** the morning of the event
2. **Say these 5 phrases out loud** 10 times each — they need to feel automatic:
   - "Can you be more specific?"
   - "Give me an example."
   - "I want to push you on this a bit."
   - "So basically..."
   - "Let's switch gears."
3. **Practice your opening** in front of a mirror or record yourself. Time it — it should be under 30 seconds.
4. **Have the backup questions open** on your phone, in case your mind goes blank
