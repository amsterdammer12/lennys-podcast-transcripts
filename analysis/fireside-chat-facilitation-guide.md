# Fireside Chat Facilitation Guide
## "AI Meets Growth" — Ash Oliver (Lovable) x Tanya Littlefield (Amplitude)
### Moderator: Dima Abramov | Amsterdam Vibe-Coding Meetup | 19:00–19:40

---

## The Big Picture

You have **40 minutes** with two people who represent complementary sides of the same transformation:

| | **Tanya Littlefield** | **Ash Oliver** |
|---|---|---|
| **Role** | VP of Growth & Digital Marketing, Amplitude | Brand + New Media, Lovable |
| **Lens** | Data, analytics, PLG, growth experimentation | Design, brand, community, creative building |
| **Company stage** | Public company ($200M+ ARR analytics platform) | Rocket-ship startup ($200M ARR in ~1 year, 8M users) |
| **Superpower** | Turning data into growth playbooks | Turning community energy into brand movements |
| **AI angle** | AI analytics powering smarter customer journeys | AI-powered vibe-coding changing who can build software |
| **Background** | 20 years B2B SaaS, 3x Amplitude customer before joining | UX design, design advocacy (Maze), community builder |

**The tension that makes this interesting**: Tanya represents *measure everything, validate with data*. Ash represents *build fast, create with AI, empower anyone to ship*. The conversation lives in the space between these two — how do you move fast AND stay grounded? How do you vibe-code AND validate?

## Conversation Arc (40 minutes)

### Phase 1: Opening & Context (5 min)

**Goal**: Get the audience oriented and both speakers comfortable.

> **Opening line** (pick one):
> - "We're sitting at this interesting inflection point where AI is changing how things get built, and data is changing how we know what to build. Tanya and Ash are living on both sides of that equation. Let's get into it."
> - "Everyone in this room has probably shipped something with AI this month. But here's the real question — how do you know if what you shipped actually matters? That's what we're here to talk about."

**First question — warm, personal, gets them talking**:

> "Before we get into the big themes — Tanya, you were an Amplitude customer three times before you joined. Ash, you said joining Lovable felt 'surreal' — you'd dreamt about it from your first prompt. What is it about these products that made you go from user to evangelist?"

**Why this works** (Lenny technique): Opens with a personal story, not an abstract question. Lets both speakers share genuine enthusiasm. Sets up the theme of product love as a growth driver.

---

### Phase 2: The Core Tension — Building Fast vs. Building Right (12 min)

**Goal**: Explore how AI changes the build-measure-learn loop.

**Q1 — Framework question (to Tanya)**:
> "Tanya, you've talked about building data-led customer journeys using AI. When teams can now vibe-code a product in a weekend, does the analytics playbook change? Or does it matter even more?"

**Q2 — Concrete example (to Ash)**:
> "Ash, Lovable sees 100,000 new products built on the platform every single day. That's an insane volume. What separates the ones that get traction from the ones that don't? Is there a pattern you've noticed?"

**Q3 — Bridge between them**:
> "So we've got Tanya saying [paraphrase her answer] and Ash saying [paraphrase his answer]. I'm curious — is there a moment where the 'just ship it' mentality and the 'measure everything' mentality actually conflict? Or do they feed each other?"

**Why this works**: Uses Lenny's framework-then-example pattern. The bridge question forces you to listen actively and synthesize — exactly what makes fireside chats come alive.

---

### Phase 3: The Blurring Lines — Product, Marketing, Community (10 min)

**Goal**: Explore how traditional roles are dissolving.

**Q4 — Observation-based (to Ash)**:
> "Ash, your role is 'Brand + New Media' — that title wouldn't have existed five years ago. You came from design advocacy at Maze, and now you're running Design Night events for Lovable. It feels like brand, community, and product are becoming the same thing. Is that intentional?"

**Q5 — Challenge question (to Tanya)**:
> "Tanya, you've spoken about product and marketing 'working together, not in parallel.' But in practice, product teams often don't trust marketing data, and marketing doesn't always understand product metrics. What's the real blocker, and how have you solved it at Amplitude?"

**Q6 — Meta-reflective (to both)**:
> "If someone in this audience is building their product with AI right now — maybe they used Lovable this afternoon — and they want to think about growth from day one: what's the one thing they should do before they write a single line of marketing copy?"

---

### Phase 4: The Amsterdam / Community Angle (5 min)

**Goal**: Make it local and relevant to the room.

**Q7 — Community question**:
> "Both of you work for companies that have strong community DNA — Amplitude's analytics community, Lovable's vibe-coding movement. We're literally sitting in one of those communities right now. What have you learned about building community that you didn't expect?"

**Q8 — Local flavor**:
> "Ash, you're based here in Amsterdam. What's different about the European builder/startup scene compared to the US? Is there something this community has that Silicon Valley doesn't?"

---

### Phase 5: Lightning Round & Close (8 min)

**Goal**: End with energy and memorable takeaways.

Announce the shift: *"Alright, let's do a quick lightning round — short answers."*

| # | Question | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| 1 | "What's a product you've recently discovered that you love — that most people here probably haven't heard of?" | Gets surprising recommendations |
| 2 | "What's the most overrated buzzword in tech right now?" | Creates a fun, honest moment |
| 3 | "Fill in the blank: In two years, ___ will be completely different about how we build products." | Forward-looking, relevant to audience |
| 4 | "Best piece of advice you've ever received?" | Personal, memorable close |

**Closing line**:
> "Thank you both — Tanya for bringing the data lens and Ash for bringing the builder lens. The sweet spot is clearly somewhere in between. Let's give them a hand."

## Facilitation Techniques (Inspired by Lenny's Podcast)

### 1. The Paraphrase Bridge
After each answer, briefly restate what you heard before asking the next person. This:
- Shows you're listening (not just reading from a list)
- Gives the audience a "subtitle" for complex answers
- Creates a natural bridge to the next question

> "So what I'm hearing, Tanya, is that [summary]. Ash, does that match what you're seeing on the builder side?"

### 2. Give Permission for Honesty
Early in the conversation, signal that rehearsed answers aren't what you're after:

> "I don't want the conference-talk version of this. What's the real answer?"

### 3. Signal Every Transition
Don't just jump topics. Tell the audience where you're going:

> "I want to shift gears and talk about community..."
> "Let me take this in a slightly different direction..."

### 4. Manage the Clock Visibly
With 40 minutes, you can't afford to let one thread run long. If someone's going deep:

> "I could talk about this for an hour — but I want to make sure we get to [next topic]. Can you give me the one-sentence version of how that turned out?"

### 5. Include the Room
At least once, turn to the audience:

> "Show of hands — how many of you have shipped something with AI tools this month?"
> (After a key answer) "Does that resonate with people here? Anyone experiencing the opposite?"

### 6. Don't Compete — Amplify
Your job is to make Tanya and Ash sound brilliant. Every word you say should serve their ideas. Aim for ~30% you, ~70% them.

## Backup Questions (If Conversation Stalls or Finishes Early)

- "What's a mistake you see teams make when they first start using AI in their workflow?"
- "If you had to start your career over today, would you still go into [marketing / design]? Or would AI change your path?"
- "What's one thing your company does internally that you think more teams should copy?"
- "Tanya — what's a metric that most growth teams track but probably shouldn't?"
- "Ash — what's the most surprising thing someone has built with Lovable?"
